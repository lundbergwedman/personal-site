My story

From reading about stocks at 13 to running a company at 19.

01 / 07 · today

Hi, I'm Gabriel

I'm 19, I live in Göteborg, and I build companies. Most days that means writing code, talking to users and figuring out what to build next, usually all at the same time.

I co-founded Repit Fitness, I have a few more projects on the way, and I serve as a cyber soldier in the Swedish Armed Forces. I mostly work with Flutter, Firebase, TypeScript and Python.

This is how I got here, one chapter at a time.

02 / 07 · age 13

It started with stocks

When I was 13 I got hooked on the stock market. What pulled me in wasn't the idea of getting rich quick, it was the question underneath: why are some companies worth so much more than others?

The more I read, the more I realised that the part I found most interesting wasn't the stock. It was the business behind it. How it makes money, why customers choose it, how it grows. At some point I stopped wanting to just own a small piece of companies. I wanted to start one.

03 / 07 · age 14

Learning to code before AI

At 14 I started programming. This was before AI could write code for you, so learning meant documentation, forums and a lot of staring at error messages until they finally made sense. It was slow, but it taught me how things actually work, and I'm grateful for that today.

Naturally, I wanted to merge my two interests. So I started building Fundaments, software for analysing stocks based on company fundamentals. The idea held up. The problem was that good financial data comes through APIs, and those APIs cost far more than a 14-year-old could afford.

Fundaments never launched, but it gave me my first real business lesson: an idea is only as good as your ability to actually run it.

04 / 07 · 2023 – now

From a notes app to Repit

Then another interest took over: training. For a long time I logged every workout in my phone's notes app. It sort of worked, but I kept wishing for an app that did it properly, without being either too basic or impossibly complicated.

So in late 2023 I started building one myself. One day my friend Olle Oskarsson joined me, and it stopped being a side project. Once he came over at 04:45 expecting to wake me up. I was already sitting there, deep in the code.

As we got closer to launch we realised that the world doesn't need another workout log. We needed to be different, so we built it out with nutrition, sleep and an AI coach that ties it all together. In early 2025 we officially incorporated Repit Fitness AB.

Today Repit is live with paying subscribers, rated 4.8 on the App Store and 4.7 on Google Play. In July 2026 we shipped Repit 3.0, our biggest update so far, and we're a team of three. repitfitness.se

05 / 07 · every week

The gym

Training has been a constant in my life for years, and it's the reason Repit exists at all. I didn't start out knowing what I was doing. Like most people, I learned by showing up, making mistakes and slowly figuring out what actually works for my body.

What the gym taught me goes way beyond lifting. Progress is boring and invisible from day to day, and obvious when you look back over a year. You can't skip the work, you have to track it to improve it, and consistency beats motivation every time. That's exactly how I try to build companies too.

06 / 07 · Swedish Armed Forces

Cyber soldier

Alongside everything else, I'm a cyber soldier (cybersoldat) in the Swedish Armed Forces. The role is about protecting systems and networks, and understanding how things break so you know how to defend them.

It's a very different world from a startup: structure, discipline and a team where everyone depends on each other. But the core feels familiar. Whether it's an app or a network, I want to understand a system deeply enough to make it better and keep it safe.

07 / 07 · what's next

More on the way

Repit isn't the only thing I'm working on. I'm building a speech app for adults who stutter, focused on guided practice in real-life situations, with privacy first. And there's Jewla, personalized jewelry made to order, my first step into physical products.

If there's one thread through all of this, it's drive. I like hard problems, I don't like waiting for permission, and I'd rather build something and learn from it than talk about it. I'm 19, and I feel like I'm just getting started.
